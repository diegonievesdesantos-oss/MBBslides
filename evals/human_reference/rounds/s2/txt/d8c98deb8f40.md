# Growth slowed because we lost customers, not because we stopped winning them: fix onboarding before hiring 30 AEs

**Governing thought:** ARR growth fell from 25% to 9.2% because churned ARR more than doubled (EUR 3.3M to 7.8M). Most of the extra churn came from new customers who never got up and running after onboarding was cut from 14 to 6 people. The sales engine still works, so the 2026 budget should go first to rebuilding onboarding and retention. The CRO's 30-AE plan (EUR 3.6M a year) should be scaled back, phased in and tied to retention targets, because it adds new customers to a funnel that is losing them and will not deliver EUR 9M of new ARR in 2026.

**Key line**
1. Churn explains about 80% of the slowdown. Net ARR added fell from EUR 10.4M to 4.8M (−5.6M): churn added 4.5M of losses, expansion fell by 1.3M and new ARR rose by 0.2M (9.4M to 9.6M). Net revenue retention fell from about 102% to about 91%.
2. Neither the price war nor sales capacity explains it. Pipeline grew 18% (EUR 38.9M to 45.9M), win rate held at 23–25%, deals won rose 10% (175 to 193), and only 17% of churned customers left for a competitor and 14% over price.
3. The cause is a broken first year after we cut onboarding. The onboarding team went from 14 to 6 (saving EUR 0.8M), median time to first value went from 21 to 48 days, first-year churn went from 12% to 26%, customers onboarded in the last 12 months account for 60% of churned ARR (EUR 4.7M of 7.8M), and 58% of exiting customers never fully implemented the product.
4. The 30-AE plan misses its own target and adds to the leak. With a 9-month ramp, Q1 hires reach full productivity (EUR 300k each) only around Q4 2026, so EUR 9M is a full-year run rate, not what 2026 will deliver. Every new customer also enters a first year that currently loses 26% of its ARR.
5. Fixing retention is the cheaper growth lever. Bringing first-year churn back to 12% would have kept about EUR 2.5M of ARR in 2025, and bringing total churn back to the 2024 rate (7.9%) about EUR 3.7M. The onboarding cut saved only EUR 0.8M a year.

**Deck outline** (one line per slide, headline only — max as many slides as the brief's max_slides)
1. Growth fell because we started losing customers, not because we stopped winning them: fund retention before adding 30 AEs
2. ARR growth fell from 25% to 9.2% as net ARR added halved from EUR 10.4M to 4.8M
3. Churn explains about 80% of the drop: churned ARR more than doubled to EUR 7.8M while new ARR stayed flat, and NRR fell from about 102% to about 91%
4. The sales engine is not broken: pipeline +18%, win rate steady at 23–25%, deals won +10%
5. The price/competitor story does not hold: only 17% of churned customers left for a competitor and 14% over price, and win rates did not move
6. Churn is concentrated in the first year: new customers churned 26% (12% in 2024) and account for 60% of churned ARR
7. The root cause is the onboarding cut: 14 to 6 people, time to first value 21 to 48 days, 58% of churned customers never implemented
8. 30 AEs will not deliver EUR 9M in 2026 (9-month ramp), while restoring onboarding protects EUR 2.5–3.7M of ARR for a fraction of the cost
9. Recommendation: rebuild onboarding now, phase AE hiring behind retention targets, and track first-year churn and time to value at board level

**Recommendation and next steps:**
- Do not approve the 30-AE hire as proposed. Approve a smaller first tranche and release later tranches only when first-year churn and time to first value are back on track. Restate the 2026 new-ARR target with the 9-month ramp built in.
- Rebuild onboarding capacity to roughly the 2024 level (the cut saved only EUR 0.8M a year) and set targets of time to first value back to about 21 days and first-year churn back to about 12%.
- Run a recovery programme for the 2025 cohort (EUR 18.2M of ARR started the year in its first 12 months) aimed at accounts that are not fully implemented, to protect renewals and bring expansion back to 2024 levels.
- Add net revenue retention, first-year churn and time to first value to the board KPI pack next to new ARR, and review them each quarter before releasing further sales hires.
