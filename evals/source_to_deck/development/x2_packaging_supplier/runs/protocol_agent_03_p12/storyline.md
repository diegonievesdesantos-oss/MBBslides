# Packaging supplier consolidation: approve Supplier B, at the real saving

**Governing thought:** Approve full consolidation on Supplier B, but at an expected saving of about €3.8M over 2026-2028 (€1.27M a year), not the €9.0M claimed, and sign only once a continuity safeguard covers its single plant.

**Key line**
1. The €3.0M a year claim is overstated. The 12% applies to €23.9M, not €25M, because €1.1M of the spend is rush freight. The case also leaves out the €0.8M switching cost and the stoppage risk.
2. Supplier B's single plant is the largest cost in the case. If its two past stoppages (9 and 14 days) happen again, they cost €4.0M over three years at full exposure, which is 46% of the €8.6M gross saving. This risk falls on every option in proportion to its Supplier B share.
3. Compared on the same basis (price, switching, stoppage risk), full consolidation still wins. It nets €3.8M over three years, against €2.08M for 70/30 and a €1.2M net cost for the status quo. It stays ahead unless stoppages cost more than €9.76M, 2.4x history.
4. Approve full consolidation, restate the budget to €3.8M, and make signing the three-year commitment conditional on a continuity safeguard.

**Recommendation and next steps:**
- Approve moving all packaging volume to Supplier B from 2026 (COO, CFO). Reject the €3.0M a year figure and restate the 2026-2028 saving to €3.8M (€1.27M a year) (CFO).
- Continuity gate before signing (CPO, Q4 2026): get a safeguard that covers a stoppage of at least 14 days, such as force-majeure compensation or safety stock at Supplier B, or a standby agreement with the incumbent. If no safeguard can be obtained, or the plant audit puts three-year stoppage exposure above €9.76M, fall back to 70/30.
- Track monthly: realised price saving against €2.87M a year, uncovered Supplier B stoppage days, and rush freight against the €1.1M of 2025.
- Treat rush freight (our own forecast errors) as a separate forecasting lever. It is not part of the supplier decision.
