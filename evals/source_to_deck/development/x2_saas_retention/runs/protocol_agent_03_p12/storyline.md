# Growth stalled on retention, not sales: fix onboarding before hiring 30 account executives

**Governing thought:** ARR growth fell from 25% to 9.2% because rising churn and weaker expansion cut net new ARR by €5.6M while new sales held. The 2026 budget should first reverse the €0.8M onboarding cut, which lost about €2.5M of ARR, and release the €3.6M AE hire only once first-year churn recovers.

**Key line**
1. The problem is retention, not new sales: net new ARR fell by €5.6M (€10.4M to €4.8M) because churned ARR rose by €4.5M (€3.3M to €7.8M) and expansion fell by €1.3M. New ARR rose by €0.2M, the win rate stayed between 23% and 24%, and pipeline grew from €38.9M to €45.9M.
2. The cause is first-year churn after the onboarding cut. First-year churn more than doubled, from 12% to 26% (mature customers: 9%), after the onboarding team went from 14 to 6. Time to first value rose from 21 to 48 days, and 58% of churned customers never fully implemented the product. Only 17% left for a competitor and 14% cited price. The €0.8M saving cost about €2.5M of ARR.
3. The CRO plan as proposed does not hold. The €9M is the full-productivity run-rate of 30 AEs at €300k each. With a 9-month ramp (assuming it is linear), a Q1 hire adds about €5.6M in 2026, which is €3.4M short, at a cost of €3.6M a year. It would also send new customers into an onboarding that loses 26% of first-year ARR.
4. Restore onboarding now and gate the AE hire. The retention levers recover up to €3.8M of the €5.6M (first-year churn back to 12%: up to €2.5M; expansion back to €4.3M: up to €1.3M). The remaining €1.8M is closed by AE hiring released at a retention gate, and most of that ARR lands in 2027.

**Deck outline**
1. Growth slowed because churn rose, not because sales slowed: restore onboarding now (€0.8M) and gate the €3.6M AE hire on retention
2. Net new ARR fell by €5.6M: churn rose by €4.5M and expansion fell by €1.3M, while new ARR grew by €0.2M
3. The sales engine held: pipeline rose from €38.9M to €45.9M, the win rate stayed between 23% and 24%, and only 17% of churned customers left for a competitor
4. First-year customers churned 26% of ARR versus 9% for mature ones and produced 60.3% of churned ARR
5. Cutting onboarding from 14 to 6 saved €0.8M, but time to first value rose from 21 to 48 days and excess first-year churn cost about €2.5M
6. The CRO's €9M is a full-productivity run-rate: assuming a linear 9-month ramp, 30 AEs add about €5.6M in 2026, €3.4M short, for €3.6M a year
7. Restoring onboarding first costs €0.8M, against €3.6M for the CRO plan and €4.4M for doing both, and removes the €2.5M churn exposure
8. Retention levers recover up to €3.8M of the €5.6M lost; gated AE hiring closes the remaining €1.8M, mostly in 2027
9. Board asks today: approve €0.8M for onboarding, reject the Q1 hire of 30 AEs, and pre-approve the €3.6M AE envelope behind a Q2 retention gate

**Recommendation and next steps:**
- **Approve** restoring the onboarding team from 6 to 14 people (€0.8M a year) from January 2026.
- **Reject** the Q1 2026 hire of 30 AEs as proposed, and remove the €9M of AE-driven new ARR from the 2026 budget.
- **Approve** an AE envelope of up to €3.6M a year. The CFO releases it in tranches at the end-Q2 2026 gate: median time to first value back to 21 days or less, and the 2026 cohorts on track for first-year churn of 12% or less. In Q1, check the productivity assumption against the ramp of the AEs hired in 2024–2025.
- **Track** every month: first-year ARR churn (target 12% or less) and time to first value (target 21 days or less). Track every quarter: expansion ARR (target €4.3M) and net new ARR (target €10.4M). Commission an account-level review to test whether the €1.3M expansion lever is real before it goes into the plan.
