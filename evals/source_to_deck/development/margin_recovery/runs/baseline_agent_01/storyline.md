# 2027 gross-margin recovery: fix electronics mix and price, not volume

**Governing thought:** Gross margin fell 1.8 pp (28.3% to 26.4%) because of mix, not volume: revenue grew 7% to €1,460M, but low-margin electronics rose from 13% to 20% of sales, which explains about 1.4 pp of the drop. The fastest route to +1.5 pp (about €22M of gross profit) is to stop diluting the mix and close the electronics price gap. Pricing and rate restoration together can deliver up to about 1.2 pp, so the plan must also cap electronics growth to close the rest.

**Key line**
1. The decline is mix-driven, not volume-driven. Revenue grew from €1,360M to €1,460M (+7.4%) and gross profit rose slightly (€384.5M to €386.1M). The margin rate still fell 1.8 pp: about 1.4 pp comes from electronics (11.5% margin) growing from 13.2% to 19.9% of sales, and about 0.4 pp from rate erosion in grocery, electronics and apparel.
2. The current plan would make things worse. The 2026 budget assumes electronics grows another 25%; at today's rates that alone costs about 0.7 pp more (26.4% to 25.7%). Freight (+€9M, +24%) is a real cost, but it sits in operating costs and is not a gross-margin driver.
3. Two levers recover about 1.2 pp quickly without touching store labour. Closing the 6% electronics price gap to the main competitor is worth up to about €17M, or 0.9 pp. Restoring FY2024 rates in grocery (+0.5 pp) and apparel (+1.0 pp) adds about €4.5M, or 0.3 pp. To reach 1.5 pp, electronics growth must be capped and redirected toward higher-margin categories.

**Deck outline**
1. We can recover 1.5 pp by 2027 if we fix electronics mix and pricing, without cutting store labour
2. Revenue grew 7% to €1,460M, yet gross margin fell 1.8 pp to 26.4%, so volume is not the problem
3. Mix explains about 1.4 pp of the drop: electronics rose from 13% to 20% of sales at an 11.5% margin, versus 26.4% for the group
4. Rate erosion explains the remaining 0.4 pp: grocery -0.5 pp, electronics -0.5 pp, apparel -1.0 pp (about €5.9M)
5. Freight rose €9M (+24%) after the March 2025 carrier contract, but it is an operating cost and must be handled separately from gross margin
6. If we keep the budgeted 25% electronics growth, gross margin falls another 0.7 pp to about 25.7%
7. Closing the 6% electronics price gap on the top 200 references is the largest and fastest lever: up to €17M, or about 0.9 pp
8. Restoring FY2024 margin rates in grocery and apparel adds about €4.5M, or 0.3 pp
9. Together these deliver up to about 1.2 pp; capping electronics growth closes the remaining 0.3 pp toward 27.9%
10. Decision requested: approve the plan, with quarterly gates on price realisation and mix

**Recommendation and next steps:**
- Approve the 2027 recovery plan built on electronics repricing, category rate restoration and electronics mix discipline. Store labour stays untouched.
- Reset the 2026 budget's 25% electronics growth assumption. Set a share-of-sales ceiling for electronics and a margin hurdle for its growth.
- Within 60 days, the pricing team validates elasticity and the revenue covered by the top 200 references. The €17M is an upper bound that assumes no volume loss across all electronics revenue.
- In parallel, renegotiate the freight contract as an operating-cost action. It is outside the gross-margin target.
