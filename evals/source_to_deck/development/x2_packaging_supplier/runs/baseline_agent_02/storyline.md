# Packaging consolidation on Supplier B: approve, but at a real saving of ~1.7 EUR M a year, not 3.0

**Governing thought:** Consolidating all food-division packaging on Supplier B is still the best-value option for 2026-2028, but the real saving is about 2.9 EUR M a year gross (12% of 23.9 EUR M, not of 25 EUR M) and about 1.7 EUR M a year once switching costs and the extra single-plant stoppage risk are counted (~5.0 EUR M over three years vs. the 9.0 EUR M implied). The board should approve it with conditions that protect continuity of supply, rather than at the headline figure.

**Key line**
1. The 3.0 EUR M headline is overstated: 1.1 EUR M of the 25 EUR M 2025 spend is rush freight billed by the incumbent, which a unit-price cut does not touch. 12% of the addressable 23.9 EUR M is 2.87 EUR M a year, and the 0.8 EUR M one-off switching cost brings the three-year net to 7.8 EUR M (2.6 EUR M a year).
2. Single-sourcing concentrates real supply risk. Supplier B runs one plant and has declared two force-majeure stoppages in three years (9 and 14 days, costing 1.6 and 2.4 EUR M of margin). The incumbent's backup needs two weeks' notice, which is too late for stoppages of that length. Assuming losses scale with B's share of volume, the extra expected loss compared with today's 30% share is ~0.9 EUR M a year, which gives ~1.7 EUR M a year risk-adjusted.
3. Keeping a 70/30 dual source is not better value. B gives 9% on 70% of volume and the incumbent 4% on the remaining 30%, for 1.79 EUR M a year gross. That is ~1.6 EUR M a year after the 0.5 EUR M switching cost and ~1.1 EUR M a year risk-adjusted. Its protection is limited, because B would still carry 70% of volume and the incumbent's cover arrives after two weeks. Over three years the full move comes out ~1.7 EUR M ahead (5.0 vs. 3.3 EUR M).
4. The 1.1 EUR M of rush freight is a separate lever that we control. 0.65 EUR M of it (5 of 12 months in 2025) is attributed to our own forecast errors, and supplier choice does not fix that.

**Deck outline** (one line per slide, headline only, within the 8-slide maximum)
1. Recommendation: approve moving to Supplier B from 2026 with conditions; the real saving is ~1.7 EUR M a year (~5.0 EUR M over 2026-2028), not 3.0 EUR M a year
2. Addressable spend is 23.9 EUR M, not 25 EUR M: 1.1 EUR M of 2025 invoices is incumbent rush freight that a price cut does not reduce
3. The gross saving is 2.87 EUR M a year, and 2.6 EUR M a year net of the 0.8 EUR M switching cost (7.8 EUR M over three years)
4. Supplier B's single plant is the main risk: 2 stoppages in 3 years (9 and 14 days) cost 1.6-2.4 EUR M each, and the incumbent's backup needs two weeks' notice
5. Risk-adjusted, the full move is worth ~1.7 EUR M a year; one 14-day stoppage would wipe out ~84% of a year's gross saving
6. Options compared over 2026-2028: full move ~5.0 EUR M vs. 70/30 split ~3.3 EUR M vs. status quo 0 (risk-adjusted, net of switching)
7. Rush freight is a separate 1.1 EUR M lever: 0.65 EUR M comes from our own forecast errors and needs fixing whichever supplier we use
8. Decision and conditions: approve with continuity safeguards, restate the business case and launch a forecasting fix

**Recommendation and next steps:**
- Approve consolidating packaging on Supplier B from January 2026, but restate the business case at ~2.9 EUR M a year gross and ~1.7 EUR M a year risk-adjusted (~5.0 EUR M over 2026-2028), replacing 3.0 EUR M a year.
- Make signature conditional on continuity safeguards from Supplier B: force-majeure and business-continuity terms, an agreed recovery plan for its single plant, and a buffer or contingency arrangement that covers the first two weeks of a stoppage, before the incumbent's 60% cover can start.
- Before exiting the incumbent, cost the option of keeping its tooling and line qualification active as a fallback. Bring it back to the CFO, along with the 70/30 alternative, if B will not accept the continuity terms.
- Separately, have the COO launch a demand-forecasting fix aimed at the 1.1 EUR M of rush freight (0.65 EUR M of it explicitly caused by our forecast errors).

*Calculation notes: 2025 invoices total 25.0 EUR M (incumbent 17.83, B 7.17), with 1.10 EUR M rush freight; excluding it, 23.9 EUR M split 70/30. Expected stoppage loss = (1.6 + 2.4) / 3 years = 1.33 EUR M a year at 100% B share, scaled by B's share of volume (an analyst assumption, not in the sources): 0.40 today, 0.93 at 70%, 1.33 at 100%. Three-year risk-adjusted figures are measured against the status quo.*
